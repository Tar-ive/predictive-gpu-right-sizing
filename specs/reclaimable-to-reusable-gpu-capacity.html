Purpose

Make the Predictive GPU Right-Sizing paper stand out without access to a live scheduler, by making its central contribution an answer to a question trace studies can address: how much over-reserved GPU capacity actually converts into capacity another job can use, and what stops the rest? The core work lands before the Nov 29, 2026 results freeze and the Dec 8 final write-up. The extensions originally planned for after the course (Phases 6–9) now start immediately as a parallel track; whatever is finished by Nov 26 can enter the paper, and the rest becomes follow-on work.

Problem

The paper's headline (about 21% reclaimable) is an opportunity estimate. The Nov 8 capacity replay shows that only 3.8% of freed GPU-hours are usable, and that replay has three weaknesses a reviewer will find quickly:

  • It shrinks only test pods, so freed GPU-hours are 1.2% of cluster capacity. Fragments rarely meet, which may understate how often they combine.
  • It tests one new-job profile (the cluster's median CPU per GPU, taken from a CPU-heavy online-inference population) and does not break down why each freed GPU-hour is lost.
  • It cannot be reproduced from this repository. The hourly, fallback, capacity and H5–H7 code is not in code/, and sections/replay.tex is transcribed from the progress report.

Other gaps are inherent to an observational trace: no latency field, no counterfactual placements and no way to resize a pod. Those can be bounded, but only with new evidence.

Solution

Put the existing replay into the repository and prove it reproduces the reported numbers. Then extend it into a loss waterfall that assigns every freed GPU-hour to one of the causes used in the ASI paper (fractional residue, multi-GPU shortfall, insufficient CPU) or to usable capacity, at realistic fleet-wide scale. This produces a finding that follows directly from ASI's own taxonomy: ASI reports fractional fragmentation is negligible because GPU sharing is rarely used, so a right-sizing policy may bring that cause back.

The unmerged scheduler replay (commit 4a75959) adds what the paper lacks on the industry side: the sizing rules open-source GPU schedulers really use, a place where a predictor could plug in, and an optimistic packing estimate that brackets the capacity replay from above. Phase 1B hardens it before anything relies on it.

In parallel, test the levers the waterfall exposes. Use an emulator for repacking, a small rented testbed to connect shortfall to latency, a natural experiment for placement risk, and online conformal prediction for the coverage gap. Every stage states in advance what result would count as support.

2.2. Implement the classifier

  • [] Implement the per-server-hour classifier below, under ideal-sharing and no-sharing bounds. The trace records servers but not GPU indices, so per-card placement cannot be observed.
  • [] Sweep new-job profiles: ASI's Figure 13 grid (1G8C, 1G12C, 4G32C, 4G48C, 4G64C, 8G64C, 8G96C, 8G128C), the cluster median, and a GenAI-median profile.
# for one server-hour and new job (g GPUs, c CPUs)
free_gpu  = server_gpus - sum(gpu_alloc_after)      # ideal sharing
whole     = floor(free_gpu)
residual  = free_gpu - whole                        # fractional residue
free_cpu  = server_cpus - sum(cpu_request_after)
n_jobs    = min(whole // g, free_cpu // c) if c > 0 else whole // g
usable    = n_jobs * g
left      = whole - usable
insufficient_cpu = left if free_cpu < c and whole >= g else 0 # after the jobs that fit
multi_gpu_short  = left - insufficient_cpu          # whole GPUs too few for g
# Invariant, also applied to the before/after differences:
assert isclose(free_gpu, usable + insufficient_cpu + multi_gpu_short + residual)

What counts as “all eligible pods”?

Default primary scope is S1. S2 is reported as sensitivity because short-lived pods have no Y_MVF and the fallback rule behaves differently for them. In S1, margins fitted on days 130–157 are applied to some earlier-admitted pods, a small look-ahead, so S1 is framed as an as-if-deployed steady state.

Yes, and this is the largest threat to the framing. ASI's SpotGPU already lends idle reserved capacity to low-priority jobs. If much of the freed capacity is already lent, right-sizing's marginal value shrinks and the headline needs to be restated. Task 2.5 measures it. Recommended, if Phase 2 is on schedule by Nov 20. It needs no emulator and could change the main message from a diagnosis to a measured remedy. It is the first thing to drop if time runs short.

Which new-job profile should be primary?

Keep the cluster median for continuity with H12, and report ASI's request grid alongside it. The median comes from a CPU-heavy population and may overstate how much CPU a typical new job needs.

Only for a narrow claim: how much capacity repacking recovers under GPU and CPU limits, relative to not repacking. Why the headline numbers are not yet usable:

  • The 16.0%, 14.6% and 18.6% values are on clusters seen in training, using a margin tuned on the test set, with no uncertainty estimate. A 1.4-point gap over 10,160 pooled pods does not establish that A3 beats VPA.
  • VPA and VPA + A3 were not run on unseen clusters. A3 is the part known not to transfer, so the 18.6% could fall substantially there.
  • Eligibility excludes left-censored pods but not right-censored ones, so pods still running at day 111 have truncated labels and hours. The Nov 8 finding that pods use less early in life suggests this may understate risk.
  • The window is short (30% of test pods warm, against 39% in the paper's test set) and uses a 20-size ladder derived from the window rather than the paper's 27 sizes. The README itself says the ranking, not the level, is the result.
  • VPA sees live sibling samples while A1 sees finished pods only, so the comparison is partly about available information.
  • Its history logic does follow the finished-before-admission rule, and VPA samples stop before admission. I found no leak of that kind.

What was verified while writing this plan

  • The ASI paper's text says fractional-GPU fragmentation “is now negligible, as GPU sharing is rarely used”, and lists fractional, stranded, insufficient CPU and network topology as causes. Figure 13 provides the request grid used in Phase 2.
  • pod_hourly has the CPU request, CPU utilization, priority and server capacity fields the plan needs. It has no GPU index and no ASW field.
  • At plan creation, the AWS session had expired, D1 and D2 parquet files were not found under ~/school, Go was not installed (Docker is), and no .env with OPENAI_API_KEY exists, so images remain placeholders.
  • The FGD simulator is Apache-2.0, written in Go, and run with bin/simon apply. Its trace columns were seen only through a mirrored README, so confirm them in task 6.1.

Alternatives considered

  • Building a live resizing system. Rejected at production scale, since there is no production cluster. A single-node demonstration through an open-source scheduler remains a separate option under Questionables, and it would show feasibility only.
  • CausalSim-style counterfactual simulation. Rejected for now: it learns from randomized trials, and the trace contains none.
  • More GPU predictors. Rejected: the NO-GO and H12 both indicate prediction is not the binding constraint.
  • Carbon framing. Deferred: with so little capacity usable today, a carbon estimate would be mostly speculative.

2026-11-13T06:12:35Z — Phase 6 stage 1 results and two findings

Phase 6 is built and running. The FGD simulator reproduces the authors' results exactly. ASI snapshots (start-of-hour, one ASW, HP pods only) replay with almost no failures, and the emulator's leftover capacity is within 1 point of the trace's own placement. Stage 1 (FGD, 720 random-hour snapshots): A1 adds 0.64 points of usable whole GPUs (95% CI 0.17–1.14). The oracle ceiling adds 9.5 points, or 18.3 points with CPU co-sizing, so without CPU co-sizing about 40% of freed whole GPUs are stuck behind insufficient CPU.

Finding for the paper: phase2 H12 counts every pod, including preemptible LP and best-effort CPU-only pods. On the same snapshots that accounting leaves 2.8% of GPUs usable, while counting only HP pods leaves 21%. The 3.8–4% reusable figure is therefore a lower bound that depends on how preemptible pods are treated, and the paper should report both. Deviation: trace arrival order is unavailable for snapshots, so seeded random orders are used.

2026-11-13T08:34:39Z — Phase 6 stage 2: placement policy comparison

Stage 2 compared six policies on 240 snapshots with 3 seeds each. GpuClustering and Random leave about 15.6% of GPU demand unplaced, so policies are compared on net usable capacity (usable whole GPUs minus unplaced demand). With original requests, all non-random policies land within 0.6 points of each other (22.4–22.9%), and perfect sizing adds 8–10 points under each of them. Sizing, not policy, decides how much capacity can be reused. FGD is the only policy that places every pod and still leaves the most usable capacity. On the uniform cluster 3f39b4b6, FGD still leads, which differs from the Stanford report; this is a hint only.
