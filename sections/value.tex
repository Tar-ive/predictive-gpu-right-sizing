\section{Business Value}
\label{sec:value}

\subsection{Framing the headline number}
The current result is that \textbf{about 21\% of reserved online-inference GPU-hours} are reclaimable while keeping under-provisioning at or below the \textbf{5\% safety constraint} ($U \le 5\%$), measured at matched risk on unseen clusters with leakage-free features (20.8\% A1, 21.0\% A3; \cref{sec:e9}). An earlier 25\% upper estimate was withdrawn after E9. If pods must be sized for their P99 or busiest hour, the figure is \textbf{about 13--14\%}. The capacity replay (\cref{sec:capacity}) adds a further and much larger discount, because only about 4\% of freed GPU-hours are usable by a new job as pods are placed today. The tiers below keep the claim honest:

\subsection{Lower GPU cost}
Per 1{,}000 reserved GPUs there are $1{,}000 \times 8{,}760 = 8.76$M GPU-hours per year. Reclaiming a share $r$ frees $r \times 8.76$M GPU-hours, or equivalently $r \times 1{,}000$ GPUs of steady capacity. We price this at three illustrative on-demand rates: $\approx$\$1/GPU-h for A10-class cards (approximate; ASI's online fleet is dominated by A10, L20 and H20 parts), \$3.43 for A100 80GB (AWS p4de per GPU), and \$3.90 for H100 (AWS P5 after the June 2025 price cut~\cite{aws2025price}, from secondary aggregators). Owned fleets have lower effective rates than on-demand, so these are upper-range translations.

\noindent For capital planning, \cref{eq:util} gives the same story: reclaiming 21\% of reservations is equivalent to raising effective utilization of the reserved pool by a factor of $1/(1-0.21) \approx 1.27$, so a fleet sized for today's demand could absorb $\approx$27\% more of the same workload before buying GPUs. At the peak-safe tier the same arithmetic gives $\approx$17\% more. The gap between the P90 and peak-safe tiers (about 70--80 GPUs, or \$2--3M per year per 1{,}000 GPUs at A100/H100 rates) is the price of never running short at peak. It is a per-service decision: strict for revenue-critical endpoints, looser for recommendation feeds. A per-segment budget (E5) lets an operator buy that safety only where it matters. Because the history rule matches or beats XGBoost in every E9 variant, capturing this does not require an ML pipeline. All of this arithmetic assumes the freed capacity can be reused, which the capacity replay shows is mostly not yet true.

\subsection{Less idle capacity}
ASI reports online-inference median SM utilization of 6\% and an allocation ratio of 68\% for high-priority work before SpotGPU~\cite{li2026asi}. Right-sizing at admission shrinks reservations \emph{before} they become idle headroom. Because recommendation pods hold most of the reclaimable capacity (33--43\% for rec-with-history), the most direct win is consolidating recommendation serving onto fewer GPUs. That consolidation has to include CPU: the clusters freeing the most GPU are those where free GPUs most often lack the CPU a new job needs (\cref{sec:capacity}).

\subsection{Faster job completion}
Capacity freed from over-reserved inference does not disappear; it returns to the pool used by preemptible training, offline inference and development jobs. In ASI, low-priority jobs already run on harvested capacity, and making that capacity \emph{reserved-free} rather than \emph{borrowed} reduces evictions; ASI's preemption-cost-aware victim selection alone cut low-priority completion time by 24\%~\cite{li2026asi}. We do not estimate a JCT gain from the trace; the simulator (E10) would.

\subsection{More reliable provisioning}
Model B addresses a separate cost: 46\% of D2 pods fail to become ready, and each failed placement temporarily occupies GPUs. Zhang and Liao~\cite{zhang2026ineffective} show readiness-aware ordering cuts ineffective occupancy from 43.64\% to 27.15\% on an Alibaba trace. A calibrated Model B used as a veto keeps right-sizing from trading GPU-hours for failed placements, and used on its own could steer risky pods (large, low-priority, scarce GPU type, busy server) to safer placements.

\subsection{Caveats on value}
Realized savings will be lower than the opportunity tiers because (i) freed fractions must be packable onto shared GPUs, and ASI shows sharing is rare due to weak isolation; (ii) $U$ is not a latency SLO; (iii) the A+B tiers rely on Model B extrapolating to unseen sizes; (iv) freed GPU is mostly stranded by missing CPU and fragmentation, so only about 4\% is usable without changing CPU requests or repacking. The value table should be read as the ceiling that justifies building the mechanism, not as a forecast, and the distance between its top and bottom rows is the value of solving GPU--CPU co-sizing.
